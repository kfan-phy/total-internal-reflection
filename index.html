Snell's law steps

Total Internal Reflection

Light from denser to rarer medium

1. Dense vs rare media

Higher refractive index n means optically denser. From dense to rare, the refracted angle is usually larger than the incidence angle.

2. Snell's law & critical angle

When r reaches 90°, the matching i is the critical angle C. If i > C, no refraction occurs and light is fully reflected.

\( n_1\sin i = n_2\sin r \qquad \sin C = \frac{n_2}{n_1} \)

i < C

Both refracted and reflected rays exist; reflection is usually weak.

i > C

TIR: no refracted ray; reflection is nearly 100%.

3. Everyday examples

Optical fibres, diamond sparkle, swimming pools looking shallower, prisms in periscopes.

What to watch

  1. 1Pick Refraction; note n₁, i and r — confirm r > i (dense→rare).
  2. 2Press i = C or Critical preset: refracted ray grazes the surface (r ≈ 90°).
  3. 3Increase i above C: refracted ray vanishes; strong reflected ray (TIR).
  4. 4Adjust n₁, n₂ and i; TIR only when n₁ > n₂ and i > C.
